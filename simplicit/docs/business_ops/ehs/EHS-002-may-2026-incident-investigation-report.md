## 1. Incident Summary

A production associate sustained a 4.5 cm laceration to the left palm while manually transferring a finished hydraulic manifold component from Deburring Station 4 to an outbound inspection cart at Plant 2. Contact was made with a residual burr on the part that was not removed during the deburring operation. The employee received emergency first aid on-site, was transported to OhioHealth Urgent Care, received 6 sutures, and was placed on restricted duty. The employee returned to full duty on June 16, 2026.

**OSHA Classification:** Recordable — Required medical treatment beyond first aid  
**DART:** 3 days away + 11 days restricted duty = 14 DART days  
**Workers' Compensation Claim:** Filed May 19, 2026 (Claim No. WC-2026-0047)

## 4. Root Cause Analysis

### 5-Why Analysis

| Why | Finding |
|---|---|
| Why 1: Why did the employee receive a laceration? | Contact with a residual burr on a finished part |
| Why 2: Why was there a residual burr on the part? | The deburring operation missed the internal bore entrance on this part |
| Why 3: Why did the deburring miss the bore entrance? | The deburring station tool path was not programmed to reach the bore entrance; manual inspection step is required but was not performed |
| Why 4: Why was the manual inspection step not performed? | Production pressure — the employee was rushing to complete the batch before shift end; the manual inspection step is documented in WI-MFG-031 but is frequently skipped per coworker interviews |
| Why 5: Why is the manual inspection step frequently skipped? | No positive-control mechanism or verification step exists in the process; supervisor did not perform required end-of-shift part audit |

### Additional Contributing Factors

| Factor | Type |
|---|---|
| Employee not wearing cut-resistant gloves during part transfer | Human / PPE compliance |
| Machine guard missing on deburring station 4 (unrelated to this event but latent hazard) | Equipment / Maintenance |
| Work instruction WI-MFG-031 at deburring station was Revision C (obsolete) — did not include updated manual inspection step added in Rev E | Document control (see also QA-003 MNC-01) |
| No engineering control (e.g., automated post-deburring inspection) exists | Engineering |

## 9. Lessons Learned and Communication

1. **Document control failures can have safety consequences** — the obsolete work instruction contributed to this event. This finding was cross-referenced with QA-003 (ISO audit MNC-01) to ensure systemic correction.
2. **PPE compliance requires active reinforcement** — periodic unannounced spot checks will be added to EHS monthly inspection routine.
3. **Production pressure is a real hazard** — supervisors reminded that schedule must not override safety procedure compliance. This incident resulted in a DART day impact that far exceeds any schedule benefit from skipping the inspection step.

Incident summary shared with all department managers at June 4 safety leadership meeting. Plant-wide safety stand-down on PPE and work instruction compliance conducted June 3.
