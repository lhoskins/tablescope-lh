## Purpose

The annual leadership offsite serves as a structured forum for the executive and senior director layer to (a) assess organizational health candidly, (b) pressure-test 2027 planning assumptions, and (c) make explicit decisions about priorities for the second half of 2026. This document captures session outputs and commitments.

---

## Day 1 — Current State and Organizational Health

### Session 1: Q2 Retrospective — "What Are We Learning?"

Facilitated by Meredith Salazar using a Start / Stop / Continue structure. Key themes:

**Start:**
- More structured cross-functional problem-solving for issues that span departments (e.g., the Line 2 scrap issue touches Manufacturing, Quality, Engineering, and Procurement but has been addressed largely in silos)
- Earlier engagement of Finance in project scoping to prevent engineering overruns before they happen, not after
- Building a bench: deliberate succession planning for the Director level and below

**Stop:**
- Letting "urgent" crowd out "important" — multiple leaders noted that strategic work gets deprioritized in favor of weekly operational firefighting
- Informal back-channels substituting for structured escalation; the Line 2 situation was known informally by multiple leaders before it surfaced formally

**Continue:**
- Weekly executive team operating cadence (Monday 8 AM) — working well
- Cross-functional CAPA process for quality issues
- Employee recognition programs

**Notable Comment (Kimberly Gonzalez, Manufacturing Director):** "We have a knowledge drain problem. When we lose an operator with 8 years on Line 2, we don't just lose a headcount — we lose process knowledge that isn't documented anywhere. The Line 2 scrap increase is partly a symptom of that."

**Notable Comment (Richard Lewis, CFO):** "The engineering project overrun on EPRJ-003 and EPRJ-007 isn't just a budget problem — it's a project governance problem. We approved those projects without adequate scope definition, and we're paying for it now."

### Session 2: Organizational Health Assessment

Leaders completed a 20-question organizational health survey prior to the offsite. Results were discussed in aggregate (no individual attribution).

| Dimension | Score (1–5) | vs. Prior Year | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity of Strategic Direction | 3.9 | +0.2 | Improving; 2027 plan needs to be more specific |
| Cross-functional Collaboration | 2.8 | -0.4 | Declining; silo behavior flagged |
| Talent Development | 2.6 | -0.5 | Significant concern; linked to attrition |
| Decision-making Speed | 3.2 | +0.1 | Minor improvement |
| Operational Discipline | 3.1 | -0.3 | Line 2 and project overruns weigh on this |
| Culture / Psychological Safety | 3.7 | -0.1 | Broadly healthy but pockets of concern |

**Facilitated Discussion:** The Talent Development and Cross-functional Collaboration scores drove the most discussion. Thomas Gonzalez shared that the exit interview data (HR-002) independently corroborates the talent development gap — "growth and advancement" is the second most cited reason for departure after compensation.

**Decision:** The leadership team agreed to form a Talent Development Working Group (owner: Thomas Gonzalez, members: Kevin Davis, Kimberly Gonzalez, Kimberly Martinez) to develop a structured career pathing framework. Deliverable: draft framework by September 30, 2026.

---

### Session 3: Culture and Values Refresh

Meredith Salazar facilitated a values discussion. Simplicit's existing five values (Precision, Integrity, Safety First, Customer Focus, Continuous Improvement) were reviewed. The team agreed the values remain appropriate but identified a gap in how they are operationalized at the front-line level.

**Specific concern raised (James Miller, EHS Director):** "We say Safety First, but the near-miss reporting culture on Plant 2's floor is not where it should be. People don't report near-misses because they worry it reflects poorly on them. That's a culture problem, not a training problem."

**Commitment:** Miller and Gonzalez (Manufacturing) will co-develop a near-miss reporting recognition program by August 31, 2026. The program will positively reinforce reporting behavior rather than treating near-misses as punitive events.

## Day 2 — 2027 Planning and H2 2026 Priorities

### Session 4: 2027 Revenue and Growth Scenarios

Margaret Green and Kimberly Martinez presented three revenue scenarios for 2027 planning:

| Scenario | 2027 Revenue | Key Driver | Probability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | $95M | Existing accounts + modest defense growth | 55% |
| Upside | $108M | Apex Aerospace expansion + new medical OEM win | 25% |
| Downside | $82M | Apex loss at renewal + continued demand softening | 20% |

**Discussion:** The downside scenario generated significant debate. Director Nair (referenced from a pre-read board discussion) had flagged concentration risk in the customer base. Cascade Defense Systems, Bolt Dynamics, Greenfield Industries, and Eagle Components together represent approximately 42% of YTD revenue. Green acknowledged the concentration but noted these relationships are multi-year and sticky.

**Decision:** Sales to develop a diversification plan targeting 3 new Tier 1 accounts in Industrial and Medical segments by end of 2027. VP Sales Green to present plan at September board meeting.

---

### Session 5: Org Design Discussion

Susan Moore facilitated a discussion about organizational structure. Three specific questions were on the table:

**Q1: Should we add a VP of Operations reporting layer between the COO and the Manufacturing/Quality/EHS Directors?**
Decision: Deferred. Team agreed the question is premature given the current attrition situation. Revisit in Q1 2027 if Manufacturing headcount stabilizes.

**Q2: Should Engineering be reorganized from a functional structure to a product-line structure?**
Discussion: Kevin Davis presented the case for reorganization into Aerospace, Defense, and Medical product verticals. Counter-argument: critical mass of engineers per vertical would be thin at current headcount. Decision: pilot a "virtual program team" model for the aerospace bracket NPI (STR-002) to evaluate project-based org effectiveness without committing to a full structural change. Review by December 2026.

**Q3: Should the company establish a dedicated Business Development function separate from Sales?**
Decision: Yes, in principle. A Business Development Director role will be posted in Q3 2026, focused on new market entry and strategic partnerships. Reporting to VP Sales. Thomas Gonzalez to develop job description and compensation package.
