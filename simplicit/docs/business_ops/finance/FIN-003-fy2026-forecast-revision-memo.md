## Purpose

This memo formally revises Simplicit Demo Company's FY2026 full-year financial forecast. The revision reflects Q2 2026 actual results, updated visibility into material cost trajectories, sales pipeline conversion data, and the status of operational recovery actions.

The revised forecast reduces full-year EBITDA guidance from **14.0%** (original plan) to **12.8%**, a reduction of 120 basis points. Revenue guidance is reduced from **$94.0M** to **$90.5M**. Specific mitigation actions are outlined in Section 4.

## 2. Drivers of the Downward Revision

### 2a. Direct Material Cost Inflation — Impact: $(2.1M) on EBITDA

The most significant driver of the revision is direct material cost inflation, running 10.3% above plan through Q2. This is primarily driven by:

- **Titanium alloys:** Cascade Alloys (SUP003) price adjustment of +18–22% due to Australian mining supply disruption. Titanium-intensive products (aerospace bracket family, defense connector series) are absorbing higher cost without full customer pass-through in the near term.
- **Specialty steel:** Harbor Steel (SUP008) and Acme Metal Supply (SUP001) have both issued Q3 price adjustments of +5–7%. Contracts with net-30 repricing clauses mean these increases take effect for Q3 purchase orders.
- **Scrap/rework premium:** The Line 2 scrap rate increase (from 2.1% to 4.8% in the April–June period) resulted in approximately $680K of unplanned material waste and rework labor through Q2.

For H2, Finance is modeling material cost inflation at +6% over plan (base case), which reflects the anticipated outcome of the Cascade Alloys repricing negotiation. If the negotiation fails, the downside scenario carries additional $(0.8M) EBITDA impact.

### 2b. Sales Revenue Shortfall — Impact: $(3.5M) Revenue, Approximately $(1.3M) on EBITDA

Q2 bookings were $15.3M vs. the $18.0M Q2 target — a 15% miss. Three deal slippages account for approximately $1.2M of the miss:

- **Bolt Dynamics Inc (CUS002):** $480K Automotive Precision order delayed to Q3 due to customer production planning cycle change. Deal is confirmed; shipment rescheduled August.
- **Greenfield Industries (CUS007):** Two precision shaft SKUs totaling $380K lost to a competitor (Midlands Machining, Cincinnati). Loss attributed to price gap of approximately 8% on commodity geometry.
- **Delta Precision Works (CUS004):** $340K medical component order delayed; customer's own FDA 510(k) clearance pushed product launch from Q3 to Q4.

For H2, VP Sales Green has presented a pipeline recovery plan targeting $25M in Q3+Q4 bookings combined. Finance considers this achievable but at the high end of probability. The revised forecast assumes $23.5M in H2 bookings.

### 2c. Engineering Project Overruns — Impact: $(0.7M) on Full-Year Spend

EPRJ-003 (Line 2 Process Optimization) and EPRJ-007 (Titanium Alloy Study) are collectively $425K over their combined budgets at the halfway point of their active periods. Both projects are tracking toward total spend of approximately $450K above original approval.

Engineering Director Davis has committed to a revised project governance process effective Q3, and no new project approvals over $50K will be granted without a finance co-signature on the scope document.

Finance has engaged KeyBank relationship manager for a proactive conversation; we believe we have goodwill for a one-quarter waiver if required, and will pursue formally if Q3 results warrant.

## 7. Conclusion and Recommendation

The revised FY2026 forecast reflects real challenges in material costs and sales execution. The mitigation plan is credible but requires disciplined execution on the Cascade Alloys negotiation and Line 2 recovery — two of the most operationally complex actions on the list.

I recommend the board accept this revised forecast as the operating basis for the remainder of FY2026, and that we commit to monthly reporting on the two leading indicators: (1) material cost per pound for titanium and specialty steel, and (2) Line 2 scrap rate.

I am available to discuss at any time.
