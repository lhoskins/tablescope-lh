## Executive Summary

Q2 2026 continued the workforce instability that emerged in Q1. Voluntary turnover in Manufacturing reached an annualized rate of approximately 22.5%, driven by compensation gaps and difficult working conditions during the Line 2 quality remediation period. Total company voluntary turnover hit a trailing-12-month rate of 13.7% in June — the highest in three years and above the 8% target. Open requisitions stand at 28 as of June 30. Compensation benchmarking confirms wages in Manufacturing and Engineering are below market. A retention and compensation adjustment program is in active development.

### Manufacturing Turnover Detail — Q1 and Q2 2026

Manufacturing experienced 23 voluntary separations in H1 2026, compared to 14 in H1 2025. The spike is concentrated in:

- **Production Operators and Specialists (Line 2 and Line 3):** 14 of 23 separations. These employees cited compensation (see HR-002) and working conditions during the scrap remediation period.
- **Tenure profile of leavers:** Average tenure of Manufacturing leavers in H1 2026 was 4.2 years — significantly higher than the 1.8-year average of H1 2025 leavers. This indicates the company is losing experienced operators, not just recent hires.

### Engineering Turnover Detail

Seven voluntary separations in Engineering in H1 2026, including two Sr Engineering Associates in March and one Engineering Specialist in June. Exit interviews (HR-002) identify compensation and growth concerns as primary drivers. Two specific individuals — Senior Engineers flagged in the risk register (RSK-004) — remain employed but are being monitored.

## 4. Compensation Benchmarking Summary

Radford Associates was engaged in May 2026 to conduct a market compensation analysis for Manufacturing Operations and Engineering roles. Preliminary findings received June 28, 2026 (final report expected July 31).

### Manufacturing (Columbus Metro Comparators, 100–500 employees, Discrete Mfg)

| Role Band | Simplicit Midpoint | Market P50 | Gap to P50 | Market P75 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Production Operator | $46,800 | $50,200 | **(7.3)%** | $54,900 |
| Manufacturing Specialist | $61,400 | $65,800 | **(6.7)%** | $71,200 |
| Sr Manufacturing Associate | $76,200 | $83,900 | **(9.2)%** | $91,400 |
| Manufacturing Coordinator | $68,500 | $74,100 | **(7.6)%** | $80,300 |

### Engineering (Columbus Metro, comparator set: precision manufacturing, aerospace/defense)

| Role Band | Simplicit Midpoint | Market P50 | Gap to P50 | Market P75 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Engineering Coordinator | $91,500 | $95,200 | (3.9)% | $104,000 |
| Engineering Specialist | $98,400 | $106,700 | **(7.8)%** | $116,800 |
| Sr Engineering Associate | $112,600 | $121,800 | **(7.6)%** | $133,100 |
| Engineering Analyst | $111,800 | $117,400 | (4.8)% | $128,600 |

**Finding:** Manufacturing and Engineering role bands are 7–9% below P50 market rates. This gap is consistent with exit interview data and explains the elevated attrition. Final Radford report will include recommended band adjustments.
