## Overview

Simplicit conducts voluntary exit interviews with all departing employees who consent to participate. In Q2 2026, 11 of 17 eligible voluntary terminations completed exit interviews (65% participation). This report summarizes themes, quotes, and patterns from those 11 interviews, supplemented by 6 responses from Q1 2026 interviews not previously reported (for a combined H1 2026 sample of 17 interviews).

| Metric | H1 2026 | H1 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total Voluntary Terminations | 51 | 29 | +76% |
| Exit Interviews Completed | 17 | 14 | +21% |
| Participation Rate | 65% | 66% | Flat |

The 51 voluntary terminations in H1 2026 represent the highest half-year total in company history. For context, the FY2024 full-year voluntary termination count was 44.

## Theme Analysis and Representative Quotes

*All quotes lightly edited for anonymization. Identifying details removed.*

### Theme 1: Compensation

Compensation was the most-cited factor across all respondents. Several interviewees noted specific awareness of market rates from competitor offers or external postings.

> "I was offered 15% more by a shop in [Central Ohio city]. I didn't want to leave — I like the people here — but I couldn't say no to that difference."
> — Manufacturing, 3.5 years tenure

> "I had a conversation with HR last year about whether there was any flexibility on my salary. The answer was basically 'not right now.' And then nothing changed. So I started looking."
> — Engineering, 2 years tenure

> "I had three friends leave in the past year. They all got pay bumps when they left. That tells you something."
> — Manufacturing, 4 years tenure

> "The pay wasn't the only reason but it was the reason I actually sent out resumes. Once I was looking, other issues became more visible."
> — Engineering, 5.5 years tenure

**HR Observation:** The consistent market awareness across departing employees suggests that Simplicit's compensation gap (7–9% below P50, per Radford benchmarking) is externally visible and is a credible contributing factor to departures. The compensation gap, not management or culture, is the primary accelerant.

---

### Theme 2: Career Advancement and Growth

Career advancement concerns were most prominent among Engineering respondents (75%) but also significant in Manufacturing (45%).

> "I didn't see a clear path to the next level. My manager is great, but there's no formal structure for how you get from [Specialist] to [Associate] to [Analyst]. It felt like waiting for someone to retire."
> — Engineering, 2.5 years tenure

> "I asked to be considered for the EPRJ-007 project lead role. I was told I wasn't ready yet but nobody explained what 'ready' looks like or what I'd need to do to get there."
> — Engineering, 1.5 years tenure

> "For hourly workers here, advancement means becoming a coordinator. There are four coordinator slots on my shift and all of them have been filled by the same people for years. There's nowhere to go."
> — Manufacturing, 4 years tenure

**HR Observation:** The absence of a formal career pathing framework is consistently cited. This aligns with the organizational health survey finding (Session 2 of offsite, EXEC-003) that Talent Development scored 2.6 out of 5. A structured career framework is a near-term intervention that does not require significant budget.

---

### Theme 3: Working Conditions

Working conditions were cited primarily by Manufacturing respondents, with explicit references to the Line 2 environment in early 2026.

> "When Line 2 was having the scrap issues, it was stressful. Parts were getting rejected, you'd do a run and it would come back for rework. Supervisors were frustrated. The whole floor felt tense for months."
> — Manufacturing, 6 years tenure

> "The equipment on Line 2 is old. We do a lot of workarounds. I spent two hours on a Monday helping fix a jam that a properly maintained machine wouldn't have had."
> — Manufacturing, 3 years tenure

> "It wasn't the physical conditions — it was the pressure. Overtime to make up for the scrap losses. Nobody said 'thank you for staying late,' it was just expected."
> — Manufacturing, 2 years tenure

**HR Observation:** The Line 2 scrap issue created a secondary human capital impact beyond direct production losses. Elevated stress, mandatory overtime, and the perceived lack of recognition during a difficult period drove several experienced operators to finalize departure decisions they may have otherwise deferred. Resolving the Line 2 scrap issue (MFG-001) is therefore also an indirect HR retention intervention.

---

### Theme 4: Management Concerns

Management concerns were cited by 6 respondents (35%), but this theme was markedly different in character between Manufacturing and Engineering.

**Manufacturing:** Concerns were primarily about front-line supervisor communication during the scrap remediation period — specifically, perceived blame shifting and lack of transparency about what was being done.

> "Nobody told us what was actually wrong with Line 2 or what the plan was. One day we heard it was the material, another day it was the machine settings. It felt chaotic."
> — Manufacturing, 5 years tenure

**Engineering:** Concerns were about project management and feedback quality at the manager level.

> "I never got clear performance feedback. My review said I was 'meeting expectations,' but I had no idea what specific things I was doing well or not."
> — Engineering, 2.5 years tenure

**HR Observation:** Management effectiveness gaps appear to be situational (Line 2 stress) and structural (inconsistent performance feedback in Engineering) rather than reflecting systemic cultural failure. Manager coaching and communication training are the recommended interventions, rather than personnel changes.

## Exit Destination Analysis

Of the 17 respondents who provided destination information:

| Destination Type | Count | % |
|---|---|---|
| Competitor manufacturing company (Central Ohio) | 7 | 41% |
| Non-competing manufacturer or industrial employer | 4 | 24% |
| Career change / different industry | 3 | 18% |
| Disclosed but outside scope | 2 | 12% |
| Declined to disclose | 1 | 6% |

**Notable:** Seven of 17 departing employees went to competing manufacturers in the Columbus/Central Ohio area. Three specific employers were named more than once: a precision machining shop in Marysville, a Tier 1 automotive supplier in Dublin, and a defense subcontractor in Grove City. These employers appear to be actively recruiting from Simplicit's workforce. HR recommends monitoring and, if appropriate, discussing with Legal whether any non-solicitation provisions apply.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Immediate:** Implement interim shift differential for Line 2/3 operators (approved, effective July 1).
2. **Short-term (Q3):** Bring compensation bands to P50 minimum for all Manufacturing and Engineering roles. Estimated cost: $380K–$510K annualized (subject to Radford final report).
3. **Medium-term (Q3–Q4):** Implement formal career pathing framework for all non-executive roles.
4. **Ongoing:** Institute a structured mid-year 1:1 culture ("check-in" not "check-up") for all employees at risk of departure, identified by manager assessment.
5. **Communication:** When operational issues like Line 2 scrap arise, Manufacturing leadership should communicate transparently with the floor workforce about root cause findings and recovery plan. Silence creates rumor and distrust.
