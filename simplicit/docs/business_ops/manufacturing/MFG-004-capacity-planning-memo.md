## 1. Purpose

This memo assesses manufacturing capacity constraints heading into H2 2026 and presents options for meeting projected H2 demand. With 17 open Manufacturing requisitions, a Line 2 recovery still in progress, and VP Sales forecasting Q3+Q4 bookings recovery to $23.5M per quarter, we are at risk of a capacity gap between approximately 5% and 12% depending on scenario.

## 3. Options Analysis

### Option A: Authorized Overtime (Existing Workforce)

**Description:** Authorize 8–10 hours of overtime per operator per week for Plant A Day and Evening shifts. No headcount addition required.

**Capacity impact:** Approximately 6–8% throughput uplift on Lines 1, 3, and 4. Line 2 overtime is limited by the ongoing quality containment requirements (inspection holds).

**Cost:** Overtime premium (1.5×) on approximately 2,200 hours/week × 8 weeks = $198K incremental labor cost for August–September period.

**Risks:**
- Operator fatigue and morale risk. Manufacturing attrition is already elevated; mandatory overtime could accelerate departures. Exit interview data (HR-002) cites overtime pressure as a contributing factor.
- Line 2 effectiveness will not benefit proportionately from overtime until the scrap rate corrective action is complete.
- Not a sustainable solution beyond 6–8 weeks.

**Recommendation:** Partially implement — voluntary overtime only, with premium incentive. Cap at 8 hours per week per operator. Authorize for August and September only.

**Risk-adjusted capacity uplift: 4–5%**

---

### Option B: Third Shift Addition (Plant A Only)

**Description:** Add a partial third shift (11 PM – 5 AM) on Lines 3 and 4 at Plant A. Would require approximately 18–22 new hires (Operators and Specialists) and one additional Supervisor.

**Capacity impact:** Lines 3 and 4 third shift would add approximately 15% additional throughput on those lines. Combined plant uplift: approximately 10–12%.

**Cost:**
- Hiring cost: 20 hires × $24.8K avg agency fee = $496K
- Wage cost: 20 FTEs × $56K avg fully loaded cost × 0.5 year = $560K
- Supervisory: 1 FTE × $90K loaded × 0.5 year = $45K
- Training / ramp: 6–8 weeks per new hire to full productivity
- **Total incremental H2 cost: ~$1.1M**

**Lead time:** Earliest third shift start date: September 15, 2026, assuming recruiting acceleration begins immediately.

**Risks:**
- Current recruiting pipeline is already strained. Adding 20 more requisitions while 17 are open will further dilute recruiter attention and drive up agency fees.
- Third shift adds supervisory complexity and has historically been harder to staff in Columbus market.
- If H2 demand does not materialize as forecast, the company is left with a cost structure mismatch.

**Recommendation:** Defer unless Q3 revenue recovery achieves ≥90% of target in July. Revisit as a Q4 option if demand sustains.

**Risk-adjusted capacity uplift: 10–12% (but delayed to October at earliest)**

---

### Option C: Outsourcing / Subcontracting (Targeted)

**Description:** Subcontract specific lower-margin, higher-volume operations to qualified precision machining partners in the Columbus/Central Ohio area. Specifically, targeting subcontract turning operations for non-ITAR, non-medical parts (Product groups P002, P004, P010).

**Capacity impact:** Frees up approximately 8% of Line 1 and Line 3 capacity currently occupied by lower-margin work, redirecting that capacity to higher-margin aerospace and defense products.

**Cost:**
- Subcontracting premium: estimated 12–18% above internal production cost for the targeted products. However, margin on these products is lower (22–26% gross), and freeing the capacity for defense/aerospace (38–44% gross margin) likely improves overall profitability.
- Qualification of subcontract partners: 4–6 weeks (Quality PROC-QA-002 supplier audit required).

**Candidate partners:** Two precision machining shops in Dublin and Marysville have been informally identified. Quality Director Robinson has reviewed their certifications (ISO 9001, IATF 16949). Formal qualification would be required.

**Risks:**
- Introduces supply chain complexity and a new supplier relationship.
- Confidentiality risk: subcontractor will have visibility into Simplicit's process specifications.
- Quality audit and qualification cannot be rushed; targeting September 1 qualification for the strongest candidate.

**Recommendation:** Pursue immediately. Outsourcing is the most cost-effective capacity solution for the near term, does not worsen the recruiting crisis, and improves product mix margin. Initiate supplier qualification process by July 15.

**Risk-adjusted capacity uplift (for higher-value products): 8–10%**

---

### Option D: Combination Approach (Recommended)

Combine elements of Options A and C:

1. **Implement voluntary overtime** at Plant A (Lines 1, 3, 4) for August–September, targeting 6 hours/week average (not mandatory). Estimated cost: $95K.
2. **Initiate subcontractor qualification** for one partner facility by July 15; target first production run in October. Estimated H2 cost: $80K (qualification) + approximately $220K in outsourced production at premium.
3. **Accelerate hiring** for Lines 3 and 4 (prioritize REQ-045 through REQ-051) with an agency surge and increased referral bonuses, targeting 10 of 17 open Manufacturing positions filled by September 1.

**Combined capacity uplift (H2):** 8–10% increase in effective throughput, with improvement concentrated in higher-margin products.
**Estimated incremental cost:** $395K
**Net margin impact (if demand materializes):** Positive, assuming defense/aerospace products fill freed capacity.
