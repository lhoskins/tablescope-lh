# POL-003: Acceptable Use of IT Resources

**Policy ID:** POL-003  
**Title:** Acceptable Use of IT Resources  
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026  
**Owner:** Chief Information Officer  
**Scope:** All employees, contractors, and third parties using Simplicit Demo Company IT resources  
**Next Review Date:** January 1, 2027  

---

## 1. Purpose

Simplicit Demo Company provides information technology resources to support business operations. This policy defines acceptable and unacceptable uses of those resources to protect the company, its employees, and its customers.

---

## 2. Covered Resources

This policy applies to all company-owned or company-managed IT resources, including but not limited to: computers, laptops, tablets, mobile phones, servers, networks, software, email, internet access, cloud services, and any systems accessed using company credentials.

---

## 3. Acceptable Use

Employees may use company IT resources for business purposes and for incidental personal use that:
- Does not interfere with work duties or productivity
- Does not consume significant network bandwidth or storage
- Does not violate this or any other company policy
- Does not expose the company to legal liability

---

## 4. Prohibited Uses

The following uses are strictly prohibited:

### 4.1 Legal and Ethical Violations
- Accessing, downloading, or distributing illegal content (pirated software, illegal media, prohibited material)
- Harassment, threats, or discriminatory communications via company systems
- Unauthorized disclosure of confidential company, customer, or employee data
- Violation of software license agreements

### 4.2 Security Risks
- Installing unapproved software, browser extensions, or plugins
- Disabling or circumventing security software (antivirus, endpoint protection, DLP)
- Connecting to public Wi-Fi without using the company VPN
- Sharing credentials or allowing others to use your accounts
- Using personal cloud storage (Dropbox, personal Google Drive, etc.) for company data without IT approval

### 4.3 Productivity and Resource Abuse
- Streaming media (video, music) for personal entertainment during business hours at scale
- Mining cryptocurrency using company resources
- Running personal business activities on company equipment
- Excessive personal gaming, social media, or entertainment use during work hours

### 4.4 Reputation and Legal Risk
- Posting content that misrepresents company positions or could damage company reputation
- Making unauthorized public statements on behalf of the company
- Participating in peer-to-peer file sharing on company networks

---

## 5. Email and Communications

Company email accounts are for business use. Key standards:

- Do not forward company email to personal email accounts
- Use professional language in all external communications
- Do not reply to or engage with phishing or suspicious emails — report them to IT
- Large file transfers must use the approved file-sharing platform (not email attachments over 25 MB)
- Electronic communications may be monitored for security and compliance purposes

---

## 6. Internet Use

Internet access is provided for business purposes. Incidental personal browsing is permitted. The following categories of sites are blocked by the web filter: adult content, gambling, malware/phishing, illegal activity, proxy avoidance, and cryptocurrency mining.

Employees who believe a legitimate business site has been incorrectly blocked may request a review through the IT Help Desk.

---

## 7. Mobile Devices

Company-issued mobile devices are subject to mobile device management (MDM). Employees must:
- Enable device lock with a PIN or biometric
- Allow MDM enrollment and remote wipe capability
- Report lost or stolen devices to IT within 2 hours of discovery
- Not attempt to jailbreak or root company devices

Personal devices used to access company email or data (BYOD) must also enroll in MDM. Contact IT for setup.

---

## 8. Privacy and Monitoring

Employees should have no expectation of privacy when using company IT resources. The company reserves the right to monitor, log, and review any activity conducted on company systems or networks for security, legal, and compliance purposes. Monitoring is conducted in compliance with applicable law.

---

## 9. Compliance and Enforcement

Violations may result in loss of IT access privileges, disciplinary action up to and including termination, and potential civil or criminal liability. Significant violations will be reported to HR and Legal.
